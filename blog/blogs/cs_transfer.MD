It’s May and a new round of computer science admission at UWaterloo just came out. Congratulations if you got in CS but if you are reading this, you are probably one of many people who got deferred to Honours Mathematics, or maybe even Geomatics.

I was one of them. I only applied to the Computer Science program at University of Waterloo but was deferred to Honours Mathematics instead. The 2023 admission cycle was extremely competitive as I failed to get admitted to my desired program.

I accepted my Mathematics offer and went in University of Waterloo with the sole intent of transferring to CS (I do not recommend). The CS transfer process is extremely competitive and the requirements as listed on the program website are:

- Have completed at least one term in the Faculty of Mathematics with a typical course load for a Computer Science major.
- Have credit for CS136 or CS146; and CS136L.
- Have a minimum cumulative math major average of 65% (calculated over all math and computer science courses) and a minimum cumulative CS major average of 70%.

The numbers may not seem high but those are the **minimum** admission requirements and the realistic cutoff are often much higher in the 90s as most students in Honours Mathematics are trying to transfer to CS. There were horror stories of students being rejected with a 99% in CS 146 but thankfully, that was during COVID where they over admitted CS students (225% more than usual). The cutoff for Winter 2024 was around 92% in CS 136 / 146.

Bare in mind that getting high grades in university is often much more difficult than high school. Averages for each course is in the 70 to low 80s so getting 90s are not an easy task. The distribution of the CS 136 midterm (766 students, mean 73.9, median 77.7) had most people fall in the 70s range. In order to transfer, you will need to score higher than most CS majors.

## How did I transfer to CS?

As stated on the website, the CS transfer comittee considers the following in making a decision:

1. Your CS major average or equivalent courses (your CS major average includes CS 136, 245, 246, 370 as well as as any other CS major courses);
2. Your Math Faculty average, which comprises of your CS and MATH courses (including ACTSC, AMATH, CM, CO, PMATH, STAT, etc.);
3. Whether you have taken any enriched CS or Math courses;
4. Your cumulative average;
5. Your workload in the term(s) you were taking CS 136/245/246/370 (or equivalent courses);
6. Your Communication Skills course grades; and,
7. Your answers to the application questions.

From talking with my peers and other students who have successfully transferred, the most important factor is your CS 136 grade (taken in 1B) and then your Math Grades (MATH 136, 138, 135, 137). Other grades are almost irrelevant but it can be used as a tie-breaker if you are very near the cutoff. One of the CS Advisors, Nomair Naeem who also happens to be on the CS transfer comittee said that a 90%+ in CS 136 will put you in a good position for a CS transfer but they will still consider all applications of 85% to 90% though it is unlikely that they will get a CS transfer.

If you want to guaratee a CS transfer, spend **A LOT** of time on academics. The topics taught in lectures should not be too hard if you spend a lot of time reviewing for it and **ATTEND ALL LECTURES!!!** (Found out the hard way on the MATH 135 final)

For Algebra courses (MATH 135, 136), there are weekly assignments and quizzes. Start the assignments early! Take advantage of your professor’s office hours and the math tutoring centre if you are struggling with any of the questions. They will often just give you the answer which guaratees a 90+ in all of your assignments. Quizzes in MATH 136 were very trivial as most of them are very similar to the set of practice problems. Be sure to familiarze yourself with all the concepts of MATH 136 and be very thorough with the practice problems as the midterm and final were almost entirely the style of practice problem questions.

For calculus courses (MATH 137, 138), there will be weekly quizzes quizzing you on concepts taught in the prior week. These quizzes are much more tricky compared to the MATH 136 ones. The quizzes consist of 3 MCs, 2 T/Fs, 2 short answers and 2 long answers. The MCs and T/F are especially tricky as it quizzes you on the nuances of the material. You will get better at MCs and T/Fs over time. Short answers and long answers, on the other hand, are fairly easy as they are often just taken off of the set of weekly practice problems. Finally, midterms and finals are just slightly harder versions of the quizzes and the TAs are very picky with their marking, be sure to write out every single step and theorem you use.

Where do I start with CS 136… the course design is awful and is designed to make you lose marks. The component of the winter offering is as follow:

- 20% Assignments (2% each)
- 5% Participation
- 25% Midterm
- 50% Final

Assignment questions are extremely vague and often require students asking for clarification on Piazza. The starter code they provide are also often scattered with bugs, making it sometimes impossible to complete. From Assignment 5 onwards, there are many edge cases that most students did not consider which resulted them in losing a lot of marks.

My recommendation for assignments is to start on the day of release. It will leave you with plenty of time to thoroughly test your code and discuss with your friends for potential edge cases if the question allows for it. Keep an eye out on Piazza as students will ask instructors for clarification and bring up potential edge cases. Additionally, read the style guide very thoroughly as it is a free 2% of your grade. It is almost impossible to score 100% on the assignment component but try aim for mid 90s in assignments.

The midterm is not difficult but you have to understand the nuances of C and be familiar with the definitions noted on the course slides. I recommend practicing writing code on paper and tracing it in your head, and redoing assignment questions as it is in a similar style as the midterm.

The final is in a similar style as the midterm but is often the most difficult component of the course. In the W24 offering, the final was fair but in W23, W22, they had to curve the final. My performance on the final was mediocre though I recommend doing leetcode easys on Tree and Linked List questions. 1/3 of the final was on Linked Lists so you should put an emphasis on that.

If you have the opportunity to take CS 146 and you are confident, you should take it over CS 136. It is a much better course even though it is quite difficult but the course average was 90 in W24.

If you follow all my tips and work hard enough, you should be in a good position to transfer to CS. Try aim for:

- 85%+ in math courses
- mid 90s in CS 136 / 146

To even better your chances of transferring, you should consider co-op sequence 3/4 instead of sequence 1 or 2 as sequence 1 & 2 has to apply and interview for jobs during the term they take CS 136 which takes up a lot of time that can be used for studying. You should also take easy electives like CLAS 104, CLAS 202, ECON 101 (bird courses) to maximize your time of studying for the core subjects.

And finally, when you apply you should demonstrate your interest in Computer Science not the job prospects of CS. I wrote that I would like to pursue a MDSAI degree after graduation and provided some additional information like a link to my portfolio website.

Good luck!
